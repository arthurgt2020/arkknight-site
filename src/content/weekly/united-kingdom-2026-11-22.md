Weekly executive threat intelligence for the United Kingdom, covering 16 to 22 November 2026. Threat level: **HIGH**. The full 27-page report was published on LinkedIn as a document post.

## Five things executives should know

1. Supply-chain attacks hit big brands. On 16 Nov an attacker abused web-testing vendor Optimizely to inject a skimming kit. Researchers name HSBC and the BBC among 178 affected sites.
2. Transport for London found a breach on 16 Nov. About 265,000 Oyster photocard holders may have had contact details, dates of birth and security answers exposed.
3. Extortion group N0n claimed seven UK online retailers in one week. One affected retail group blamed a shared search vendor, not its own systems.
4. Core enterprise software is under attack. Google says a suspected China-nexus group exploited a VMware vCenter flaw as a zero-day from early September. CISA listed nine exploited flaws this week.
5. Attackers tampered with refrigeration controllers at UK meat suppliers and a London wholesaler on 2 Nov. The NCSC called the impact minimal, and no one has been formally blamed.

Regulators are reacting: the FCA says October brought its second-highest monthly count of reported cyber incidents in five years.

**Bottom line:** a UK public body or major company will likely confirm a disruptive cyberattack by 23 Dec (60 to 75%, moderate confidence).

**What to watch:** N0n naming four more retailers, Optimizely's account of the attack, and any NCSC alert on vCenter or Copeland controllers.

Every claim in the report is sourced and was checked twice: 81 sources, each Admiralty-rated.
